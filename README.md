# Everforge

A mobile-first crafting and battling game. Fight through four regions, deconstruct what you find into base materials and essences, forge gear from patterns and blueprints, and bind essences into it. It runs entirely in the browser, with no build step and no dependencies. Progress is stored in `localStorage`; Settings can export and import a save code.

## Run it

Open `index.html` in a browser, or serve the folder with any static server (`python3 -m http.server`). It loads three fonts from Google Fonts and falls back to system fonts offline.

## Conventions

Cinder Hollow's regular enemies cover all three movement types: the wandering Ember Imp, chasing Nightshade Witch and stationary Slag Sentry. The sentry is a Stone construct weak to Ice and Arcane, resistant to Fire, with a heavy Crucible Hammer every fourth turn. It drops Ember Coal, Golem Cores and Stone Plate; new maps, daily expeditions, bounties and the Codex pick it up from the enemy table. Existing maps retain their preselected enemies. The wandering Anvil Crawler is the Hollow's Metal construct: weak to Arcane and Ice, resistant to Fire, with a two-hit Pincer Clamp every third turn; it drops Golem Cores, Ember Coal and Ember Treads. Every region's hand-made roster now covers its whole biome pool (Woods: wood, poison, fire, swift, metal; Pass: ice, swift, stone, arcane, metal; Hollow: fire, poison, metal, stone; Spire: arcane, holy, swift, shadow), and `tools/sim.js` fails if a pool element has no authored enemy. The three creatures that closed the gaps: the **Kindle Fox** (Woods, Fire, chases, weak to Ice, resists Fire, a heavy Tail Flare), the **Cairn Seer** (Pass, Arcane, stands still, weak to Holy, resists Arcane and Ice, heals itself with Glyph Mend) and the **Anvil Crawler** (Hollow, Metal, wanders). The Woods' Briarling is now Poison (its sap closes the wound it opens) and its Mossback Stag is now Swift (weak to Stone and Fire, resists Swift).

- The scripts are plain (non-module) scripts that share one global scope, so they work from `file://` without a server. **Load order matters**; it is the order of the `<script>` tags in `index.html`. Each file may use anything defined by files above it at load time, and anything at all inside functions at call time.
- All game state lives in the single global `S` (see `freshState()` in `state.js`). After changing it, call `save()` and `render()`.
- **Creature art (`js/art/*.js`).** `portrait(id,o)` draws a hand-drawn `PORTRAITS[id]` if there is one; otherwise it composes the enemy from `ENEMIES[id].art={b,p,c,skin,glow}`: a body per family (`BODY`: `beast` (side view), `undead`, `construct`, `elemental`, `humanoid`) plus overlay parts (`PART`: horns, antlers, mane, spikes, armor, crown, halo, wings, tail, flames, frost, crystals, vines, runes, hood, blade, axe, staff, chains, orbit, shell, tusks; `z` -1 draws behind the body, 1 in front). Bodies expose anchors (head, shoulders/back, chest, hand, tail) that parts attach to, and everything is tinted from the element's `FAM` colour (`art.c` overrides it while the enemy fights as its own element; an elemental affix retints it). **Shared look** (every portrait, hand-drawn ones too): `c.m`/`c.d` are top-lit gradients that drift in hue (flat versions are `c.M`/`c.D`), anything drawn in the eye colour `c.e` blooms through a glow filter, `EL_FX` gives each element an accent `c.a` and eye colour, `AMB` plays an element ambience around the creature (embers, snow, bubbles, rays and stars, shadow wisps and a vignette, leaves, sparks, orbiting runes, wind, dust), creatures breathe or hover (`FLOATS`, elementals) over a ground shadow, magic and rare foes get a blue or gold rim light (rares also sparkle) and bosses an element-coloured rim and pulse. Particles are CSS animations (`.amb-*` in `styles.css`) with a random phase from a seeded `rng(hash(id))`, so they never touch game randomness and the reduced-motion rule stops them. A composed body also takes a pattern (`PAT`: stripes, spots, scales, glowing cracks, stars, rot, facets, bands, swirl) in `art.pc` or its accent, masked to its silhouette; a body is split into `s` (the silhouette) and `f` (face and details drawn over the pattern). The Codex grid passes `lite` to skip the ambience on its small tiles. Affixes show on the portrait through `AFX_ART`: on a composed body an affix adds a part (Ironclad armour, Hallowed halo, Burning flames, Frostbound frost, Arcane runes, Thorned spikes, Regenerating vines); anything the body cannot wear, and every affix on a hand-drawn portrait, becomes a small frame mark, and Elusive adds an afterimage. **Adding a regular enemy** is a data entry: give it an `art` line and translations (name, plural, intro, lore, special name and tell, `hit|<verb>`); drops should come from the zone's existing items so the economy and `zoneRare` stay put. Each region has six or seven hand-made regulars (two of each movement type, the Spire keeps six), the new ones at weight 2, plus ten **wild creatures** (see `monsters.js`); re-run `tools/sim.js --enemy <id> --all` against the zone's other regulars, and `tools/sim.js --wild` for the region's wild creatures. **Element coverage:** every element of a region's biome pool (`BIOMES[ZONE_BIOME[zone]].els`) needs at least one authored (non-boss, non-generated) enemy of that region; `tools/sim.js` prints the coverage and exits 1 on a gap, with a documented exception map (`EL_EXCEPT`) for the rare deliberate omission. The current exceptions are none: the enemies outside their pool are additions, not gaps (Woods: holy Glowcap Sprite, shadow Barrow Ghoul; Pass: shadow Ice Wraith; Hollow: shadow Ash Revenant; Spire: metal Orrery Construct).
- The Gear screen's hero (`heroArt()` in `hero.js`) is composed from the seven live `gearOf()` slots: worn armor uses material/trait colors and overlays, held items reuse `art()`, and `heroMini()` draws a simpler equipped figure for the map and HUD. The surrounding slot buttons keep the normal item-sheet actions. This is derived art, with no extra save fields or image downloads.
- Weapon-form and shield abilities live in the `ABILITIES` table (`data.js`); `ability(key)` in `battle.js` runs them, cooldowns are per battle in `B.cd`, and `effects()` reads the same table for item text.
- Item sheets, known forge previews and forge results share `itemProperties()` (`render/items.js`). Consumables lead with **When used**: actual HP restored, individual cures and damage above the combat threshold; equipment shows its final stats and effect badges. Trait numbers are labelled as strength, and `traitTip(t,v,item)` (`gear.js`) explains their effects in that specific item. Materials describe possible effects after forging. **Stat calculation** stays collapsed until opened: `statParts()` (`crafting.js`) exposes each unrounded contribution, priority, form multiplier and tier/legendary bonus alongside the actual rounded `statsOf()` totals, Medicine healing bonus and consumable damage rule. Satchel and recipe tooltips also show actual consumable use effects. Icons use `traitIc()` (`art.js`), with distinct Sharp and Heavy symbols; effect badges keep hover/focus/tap explanations and turn, hit and cooldown markers.
- Damage model (`data.js`, used by `strike`, `foeHit` in `battle.js` and `pstats()`): a blow does `base²/(base+armor)` where `base` is half the strike power (`Attack + Magic x MAG_W`) and `armor` is the enemy's Defense (less Tempered shred, less what Heavy/Lunge/Channel ignore), so armor takes a share of a hit but never all of it. An enemy hit is cut by `redOf(def)=def/(def+DEF_K)`, after your element resist, then by Stone's flat `dr`, but resist, Defense and Stone together never cut it below `HIT_MIN` of its raw damage; Heat 5 enemies bite through `HEAT_T.bite` of your Defense first. Guard (`GUARD_T`) cuts all hits in the enemy action by 60%, blocks new on-hit statuses, and grants non-stacking Braced: +30% damage on the next Attack or ability action, including every follow-up, off-hand strike and multi-hit ability. It does not cleanse existing statuses or boost consumables/ripostes. Parry uses `parryChance(B,P)`: `PARRY_T.base` (65%) plus 5 percentage points per special-training rank (up to 3), up to 5 points from Speed (0.5 per point), and a form modifier (+5 for blades, spellblades and claws; -5 for lances and warstaves), capped at 90%. A known special gives rank 1; Study adds a rank, separately for each boss form. Persistent ranks live in optional `S.codex[id].timing.sp` / `.sp2` fields, so older saves need no version bump. Failed parries still take 25% extra damage and lose the next guard; off-balance parries show 0%. Change these constants, not the formulas, and re-run `tools/sim.js` (`effects()`, the Heat text and the rules paragraph in the Codex fill their numbers in from the same constants).
- New maps use a randomized growing-tree network, open chambers and shortcut loops instead of a prescribed northward corridor and two fixed wall bands. Start and boss columns vary, the boss remains at the top behind a stationary gate guard, and the camp occupies connected ground beside that gate. POI counts and the 40-step torch budget stay unchanged: the added space offers route choices and optional exploration rather than multiplying loot. Map validation uses each saved map's own bounded dimensions, preserving existing maps, fog, POI indices, open battles and daily runs without a save-version bump.
- Heat (`HEAT`, `HEAT_T` in `data.js`) is stored per zone in `S.heat[zone]={max,sel}`; a map remembers the heat it was generated with (`map.heat`) and `heatNow()` reads it. Every Heat level adds `HEAT_T.hp` HP and `HEAT_T.atk` Attack to all enemies (so Heat 5 is +HEAT_T.hp x5 HP and +HEAT_T.atk x5 Attack), on top of the per-level rules in `HEAT`. The daily lives in `S.daily` (not `S.maps`); while `S.run==='daily'`, `curMap()` returns its map and `S.zone` is the daily's zone, restored from `S.daily.back` by `finishDaily()`. Daily maps come from `genMap({seed,...})`, which uses the seeded `rng` instead of `Math.random`.
- Bosses can carry a data-driven `phase` (see `archon` in `data.js`): below `at` of max HP they spend a turn changing form, and `foeOf(B)` returns the current form's fields (element, weak, resist, special, status). Use `foeOf(B)` rather than `ENEMIES[B.id]` for anything that depends on the form.
- Adding a zone: append to `ZONES` and give it enemies, a boss and a `terrain()` look in `art/map.js`. Unlocking, the mimic scaling (`MIMIC_HP`/`MIMIC_ATK`), bounties and the daily pick it up; a boss kill opens the next zone (a daily boss kill too), so a reload never changes what is open.
- **Wild creatures and random regions (`monsters.js`).** A **biome** is a theme: its elements, bodies, parts, patterns, colours, words and region names (`BIOMES`). The four hand-made regions use `forest`, `tundra`, `ember` and `astral`; the Rift rolls from all eight (plus `fen`, `ash`, `bone` and `deep`). **Every region has ten wild creatures** (`genRoster`): a body and parts from its biome, an element, a name from the biome's words, a special from the shared pool `GEN_SP` and **one or two affixes of its own** (30% rare). They are the same in every playthrough (the seed is the region's id), they are rare-grade, so the map shows them with a frame, and they weigh 1 against the hand-made regulars' 2-3 (about 40% of a region's encounters). Their stats are the region's band with the rarity multiplier divided out, so a wild creature lands where a regular lands, plus its affixes. **A wild creature's affixes are its own** (`speciesAfx` in `affix.js`): the map never rolls more for it, and because they are part of the creature they drop **no extra essence** and pay **no elite embers**, so a region's economy does not move. Mastering one is worth 1 research point, not 2 (there are many). **The Rift** is a random region past the Spire: `riftMake(seed,depth)` rolls a biome, a palette, a name, ten wild creatures and a master (three affixes), and the whole region is rebuilt from the seed in the save, so a reload shows the same region. `S.rift={depth,best,seed,biome,name,pal,cleared}` holds it; `riftDescend()` goes one depth deeper (the tier grows 18% HP and 12% Attack per depth), and the Rift is never picked for a daily. A procedural name, lore line or intro has no dictionary entry: it stays English, and `tools/i18n-check.js` skips it (`E.gen`); the strings live in `PROC` so the translation tour can skip them too.
- Maps are tile grids (`GRID` 13x17 in `data.js`; existing saved 9x13 expeditions retain their dimensions): `m.g` terrain rows (`.` floor, `,` brush, `#` rock/tree, `~` water; the last two block), `m.pois` (`{t,x,y,r,done,...}`; `r` is depth 0-5 by walking distance from the start, 6 = the lair; a seeded daily map adds the same pre-roll fields per POI as it did per node), `m.log` (map log lines, last 3 shown under the map), `m.sn` seen flags, `m.mem` last-seen POI types, `m.pos`/`m.prev`. `step(x,y)` moves (4-way), drains the torch, reveals (`reveal`, Chebyshev light radius `TORCH.rad`, `TORCH.dim` when the torch is out) and calls `enter(poi)`, the single resolver for battles and events. At torch 0 each step rolls an ambush (`TORCH.amb`, seeded per step on the daily). An ambush (a dark one, a robbed traveller's friends, a mystery tile, or a hunter that lunges at you) lets the foe strike once before your first action, and the spear's opening jab is skipped; a defeat from that blow goes through the normal path. `torchUp(n)` refills (camp full, shrine prayer, campfire rest); Heat 5 drains `TORCH.h5` per step. `fallBack(m)` is the boss flee. **Roaming enemies:** every regular enemy has an `mv` in `ENEMIES` (`still`, `wander` or `chase`; `ROAM` in `data.js` holds the tunables); genMap picks each fight/elite POI's enemy up front (`p.e`) and copies the movement type to `p.mv` (elites keep their species movement, but act more slowly; the gate guard and boss never move; none start within 3 tiles of the start). After each step that starts no encounter, and never on the first step, `foesAct(m)` lets every live mover act once in `m.pois` order: wanderers step to a random free neighbour (or stay, 40%); a chaser notices you within 4 path steps (6 with the torch out, +1 at Heat 3+; elites within 2), then follows a BFS path until you are over 8 steps away; elites act every other step. Enemies never enter rock, water, the start, gate or lair tiles or any non-enemy POI, and never share a tile; an enemy that steps onto you starts the fight through `enter(p,"it",from)`. The depth scaling stays `p.r` from generation. State lives on the POI (`x`,`y`,`aw` aware, `dz` daze steps, `pa` elite parity, `ms` last step it moved), so saves need no version bump; a daily seeds its wander rolls per step (`m.seed`+`m.steps`) and survives a reload. A new map is saved as soon as it is made (`curMap()`), so a reload does not reroll it. Fleeing a map enemy (`slip(B)`, `B.pi`/`B.by`/`B.ef` say which, who moved and where it came from) leaves it alive and dazed for `ROAM.daze` steps and puts you back on the tile you came from (or leaves you in place if it walked into you). A foe you fled from (or a boss you fell back from) keeps the HP it had left on its tile (`p.st`: the HP, and whether it had changed form), so you cannot chip it and meet it at full HP again; `startBattle` restores that state, and it lives in the saved map, so it survives a reload. Maps already in a save from before this keep their `mv:"still"` enemies; new expeditions roam. Only lit tiles show live enemies: crossed swords for stationary foes, footprints for wanderers, and a pursuit arrow for chasers. Icons read the POI movement, including stationary gate guards and old maps; movement names and descriptions appear in the map key and tooltips. Rarity frames, affix pips and hunting/daze badges stay visible. A remembered enemy tile uses generic faded crossed swords, without hidden movement, rarity or awareness information; no save change is needed. Map tiles use the classes `.tl`/`.tls` (not `.tile`/`.grid`, which are taken).
- **Affixes (rarity tiers, `affix.js`; tables in `data.js`).** A map enemy (fight, elite, ambush; never a boss or the mimic) is **normal** (no affix), **magic** (1 affix) or **rare** (2 affixes, 3 from Heat `RAR_CH.k3` = 4). Rolls: an elite tile is magic or rare, with the rare share `RAR_CH.rare` = 15% + 5% per Heat + 3% per depth row `r` (capped at 60%); an ordinary fight turns magic from Heat 2 (10%, +5% per further Heat); dark ambushes roll like fights. `rollFoe(R,{elite,heat,row,mv})` returns `{rar,afx}` and `rollAfx` picks the affixes; both take the rng as `R`, so the daily pre-rolls them per tile from its own seeded rng (`hash(seed:r:x,y)`, so the other pre-rolls are untouched) and a walked daily meets the same foes whatever `Math.random` does. POIs store `p.rar` and `p.afx` (ids), rolled once so fleeing and returning meets the same foe; mystery ambushes and unrolled old maps roll when entered. `startBattle(id,{rar,afx})` (or `elite:true` to roll an elite) applies them with `applyAfx(B,E,rar,afx)`: **`RARITIES`** gives HP and Attack multipliers (magic x1.35 HP, x1.15 Attack; rare x1.55, x1.2; `def` adds Defense), each affix's `m` adds its own (elemental +1 Defense, Regenerating x0.9 HP, Elusive and Splitting x0.85 HP, Swift-footed x1.05 Attack), and a battle stores `B.rar`, `B.afx`, `B.title`. Name: the first affix's prefix plus the species ("Vampiric Frost Wolf"); a rare adds its second affix's title ("Umbral Frost Wolf the Bloodthirsty"); the HUD clamps the name to two lines. Colours: magic blue and rare gold, the Greater and Pure grade colours. Two affixes never combine when they share a group (`g`, space-separated: one elemental; one of Warded/Regenerating/Elusive; one of Vampiric/Regenerating/Warded; one of Splitting/Volatile) or the same essence; **Swift-footed** is only rolled for species that move. `B.el` and the family resist come from the elemental affix, if any (`elTr(B)`), otherwise from the species; the old elite status chance (`ELITE_INF`) follows that affix.
- **Affix table** (`AFX`, numbers in `AFX_T`; each hook is one helper in `affix.js` called from the combat flow, and every effect has a log line and a tip on its HUD chip): the eight **elemental** affixes keep the old elite behaviour (Burning/Fire, Frostbound/Ice, Venomous/Poison, Hallowed/Holy, Umbral/Shadow, Ironclad/Iron, Fleet/Swift, Arcane/Arcane: fights as that family, resists it, loses any weakness to it, +1 Defense, 25% status on hit for Fire, Ice, Poison and Shadow; drops that essence). The behaviour affixes, with the essence each drops:

  Rewards: every affix drops an affixed raw when the foe falls (`afxDrop`: the species' first `DECON` raw with that affix, `boar_hide~vamp`; deconstructing it always gives that affix's essence, see Materials), graded by `rollGrade(zone,heat,x,up)` with `up` = `RARITIES[rar].gs` grade steps better (a fraction is the chance of one step), never above the best grade the zone's `GRADE_ODDS` can roll. Embers: `EMB.rar` (magic 5, rare 8; the old elite paid 5). **`gs` is 0.5 (magic) and 1 (rare), not a whole step and two:** with whole steps essence income jumped far past the 20-25% budget (`tools/sim.js --econ-only` at Heat 3, `ess+sv`: hollow +41%, spire +37%); with these values the total (`ess+sv`) stays within about +25% at Heat 0 and 3 and embers within 6%. The direct `ess` column rises more (affix essences are its main source). Raise `gs` to 1 and 2 for the spec'd steps and re-run the sim. The Codex's Affixes list reads `S.afx[id]={s,b}` (fights met in, enemies beaten; `noteAfx`), so an entry opens when you first meet the affix. `S.afx` came with save v13; saves are latest-only (see Save versioning below), so an older save is not loaded at all. The Heat text and the Codex's affix rows fill their numbers from the same constants.
- **Materials, deconstructing and patterns (save v17; tables in `data.js`, logic in `crafting.js` and `economy.js`).** Monsters drop food, potions, herbs, relics and raw finds, never weapons or armor (old saves keep theirs). **Deconstruct** (`deconstruct(id)`, the item sheet's button and the Gear tab's *Deconstruct all raw finds*, `deconAll`, which skips food, potions, relics and anything a secret still wants) breaks anything that is not gear, crafted, an essence or a base material: a raw in `DECON` gives its base material (`boar_hide` -> 2 `boar_leather`), and every trait except the material's base trait (`MAT_T[type].tr`) gives that many Faint essences per Plain point (`FRAG.step` grades below the item's grade) plus a `FRAG.ch` per point chance (capped at `FRAG.cap`) of one essence at the item's own grade, and embers as salvage pays them. Food, potions, herbs and relics give fragments only. `deconYield(it)` lists all of it for the sheet. **Base materials** (`MATS`: Leather, Cloth, Ingot, Timber, Stone, Crystal, each with a zone variant that adds one trait) are graded like ores (`gradable` knows the `timber` kind too). **Affixed variants:** an id `<base>~<affix>` (`boar_hide~vamp`, `boar_leather~vamp@4`, `iron_sword~vamp`) is the BASE item with the affix prefix in its name and `AFX_RAW` more of the affix's trait (`baseItem`, `afxOfId`, `plainId` strips grade and affix; `iname` composes the translated name). An affixed raw deconstructs into the affixed material and always gives `FRAG.afx` fragments of the affix's essence plus an `FRAG.afxCh` chance of a full one. **Patterns** (`PATTERNS`, known from the start, the Forge's Patterns panel, `renderPatterns` with UI state in `patSel`): each slot takes some material types; the result is the sum of the materials' traits times their slot weights plus the pattern's own traits (under `PAT_MIN` dropped), a new crafted piece (`c_` id from the pattern and the materials, `pat` set, named from its top traits and the pattern's noun). A pattern's **standard recipe** (`std`, exact Plain materials) makes the existing BASE item instead (`iron_sword`), so secrets, recipes and old saves keep their ids; graded materials make it at the lowest grade among them (`iron_sword@4`: graded gear uses the same `@g` ids and `mkGraded`), an affixed one adds its affix. Pattern forging never fails and pays `SHAPE` smith and skill XP (`shapeIt`; `S.stats.shaped`). **Blueprints** (`BLUEPRINTS`: the ten named pieces, each tied to a creature `e`) are Codex facts (`S.codex[e].bp`, outside the knowledge total so mastery is unchanged): Study can reveal one (`unknownFacts`), mastering the creature teaches all of its blueprints, each pays `RP.bp`, and the Codex sheet lists them. A blueprint always makes its named item (lowest grade, first affix). **Salvage** is now for gear and crafted pieces only: half their materials back (`salvageMats`: a pattern piece's own, a plain BASE piece's standard recipe or blueprint `m`, a crucible craft those of its inputs), essences never beyond what was bound in (`salvageYield`, `essIn`; plain gear gives none), and embers. The daily boss's and the grateful traveller's rare find is an affixed raw of the region, a grade better (`rareFind`). Numbers (`FRAG`, the raw drop chances) were set with `tools/sim.js --econ-only`: embers stay within about 5% of before; essences run 0-35% under the old salvage values depending on zone and seed, which is the intended trade for materials.

  Once every technique is known `rpGain` pays nothing. **Techniques** (`S.techs={id:order}`; `techState`: learned, available (prerequisites and requirement met) or locked) are bought in the Forge's Research panel (`renderResearch`: an icon tree, a detail strip with a Learn button, and Study a material). `craftMiss(items)` is the one gate for a craft (the crucible hint, the Forge button's tip, the click, the Recipes tab and the sim all use it): Binding is an item plus one essence; **Layering** (any crafted input), **Compounding** (anything beyond one item and essences, or two essences), **Tempering** (3 or more inputs; the crucible slots still come from Smith level) and **Alloying** (a `FUSIONS` pair) are needed on top of it. A refused craft says why in the Forge button's tip (`Needs the Alloying technique (Research).`) and a toast; before Research opens it only says to start simple. Elsewhere: **Refining** is the `upgrade` unlock's `when` (and `upgrade()` checks it), **Mending** gates `reforge()` and its sheet button, **Precision I/II** take `PREC` points (5 and 5) off every forge's failure chance (`combine`, never under `FAIL.min`; the forge odds tip says so), **Masterwork** lets `SECRETS` succeed: without it the exact ingredients make an ordinary piece with its own id (`key+"|plain"`) plus an insight, so the legendary can still be forged later.   **Discovery.** *Study a material* (Research panel): spend one essence of a family (the lowest grade you hold) to reveal a step of an undiscovered reaction it takes part in. `S.rxh[key]` lists the traits of that reaction you know: the first study shows the family (`Fire + ???`), the next its partner, the next starts another reaction; a family with no reactions can be studied once for its RP. The Recipes tab shows these as dashed partial reactions. *Insights*: a craft that holds one side of an unseen reaction rolls `insightRoll` (`INSIGHT.ch` = 35%, a hash of the recipe and the forge count, so the same state always answers the same) and, on a hit, picks one such reaction by hash, logs `The Iron stirs in the crucible... (insight: Iron reacts with something hot.)` and records that side in `S.rxh` (`VAGUE` words the partner). A craft with over half of a secret's ingredients logs `This feels close to something greater` once per secret and surfaces its rumor in `S.hints`; the exact ingredients without Masterwork say it needs that technique every time. All of it is `insights()`, called by `forgeIt`.

- **Codex facts follow the creature.** `loadSave()` prunes every `S.codex[id]` entry to the enemy's current facts: `weak` and `resist` down to what the creature (and, for a boss, its second form) has now, and `drops` down to the ids it drops now. An enemy that changed its element (the Woods' stag and briarling) would otherwise leave a stale weakness in the save, which `know()` counts: it inflates `have`, can mark the enemy mastered when it is not, and shows the wrong weakness in the Codex. The prune is idempotent, so it needs no save bump.
- **Localisation (English, German, Korean; `i18n.js`, `lang/`).** English is the source language and the dictionary key: every shown string is written once in the code as English and looked up with `tx("Text with {placeholders}",{vars})`. A missing translation falls back to English, so nothing breaks while a language is incomplete. Game data (`ENEMIES`, `BASE`, `SECRETS`, `UNLOCKS`, lore, item names inside saves) stays English and is translated where it is drawn (`tx(E.name)`, `iname(item)`), which is why the language switches live without a reload and why saves and secrets never depend on a language. Helpers: `txn(one,many,n,vars)` (plural pair; a translation is `"singular¦plural"`, or one form for a language without plurals), `txc(ctx,word)` (a word in a context, stored as `"ctx|word"`: `trait|`, `adj|`, `noun|`, `gender|` (German), `fam|`, `form|`, `kind|`, and `hit|<verb>`, one full template per enemy attack verb with `{name}`, `{n}` and `{g}`), `trn(trait)`, `famN(f)`/`famL(f)` (a family as a name or inside a sentence), `lc()` (lower-case, but never for German nouns), `iname(item)` (the shown name: base items and essences from the dictionary, crafted items composed from their adjectives and noun, German with the adjective ending of the noun's gender), `bname(B)`/`btitle(B)` (an affixed enemy's name). Tables whose fields are shown in many places use getters (`HEAT`, `STATUS`, `STAT_NAMES`, `ABILITIES`, `SKILLS`, `DAILY_TWISTS`, `RARITIES`, `NODE_INFO`) so every reader gets the current language; do the same for a new table, and put numbers into text as `{placeholders}` with the values passed at the call (`UNLOCKS[..].i.v` carries them for intro cards). A **Korean** template can name a particle after a placeholder, `{name|은|는}`, `{name|이|가}`, `{name|을|를}`, `{name|과|와}`, `{name|으로|로}`: it is picked from the last syllable of the value. German avoids articles before names ("Dornenkeiler fängt Feuer") because the name has no gender in the table. Static markup in `index.html` uses `data-t` (the element's HTML is the key, or `data-t="howto.map"` for a long paragraph) and `data-ta="aria-label placeholder"`; `applyStatic()` rewrites them on a language change. The choice is kept outside the save (`localStorage` key `everforge-lang`, picked from the browser language at first launch) and is changed in the Codex tab. Stored text (battle log lines, an open event, the last forge's log) stays in the language it was written in until the next one. **Adding text:** write it as English with `tx(...)`, add the entry to every `lang/*.js`, and run `node tools/i18n-check.js`. **Adding a language:** add it to `LANGS` in `i18n.js`, add `lang/xx.js` and `lang/xx-data.js`, load them in `index.html`, and copy the keys from `de.js` / `de-data.js` (the check lists whatever is missing). Hangul needs a Korean font: `applyStatic()` loads Noto Sans KR for `ko`, and `styles.css` has `:lang(ko)` rules.
- **Save versioning.** Saves are latest-only: only a save of the current version (`v:17`, key `everforge-v17`) loads, and any other save starts a fresh game. When the save shape changes, bump `KEY` and `v`; older saves start fresh. `loadSave()` only validates and clamps a current save (extend `sane()` if new required fields are added).

Hint scrolls come from chests, shrine offerings, riddles, travellers, boss first kills, bounties and the daily. - Map events live in `ENC[type]={make(rv,hl),pick(choice,ev)}`; `rv` is four rolls pre-rolled per POI (`poi.rv`) in the seeded daily map, plus four more (`rv[4..7]`, from `poi.gr`) that roll the grade of whatever the event gives. - Embers (`S.embers`, tunables in `EMB` in `data.js`) come from fights (`fightEmbers`), deconstructing, salvage and selling. The peddler is a shop (`merchant(sh)`; the stock is pre-rolled per POI as `poi.sh` on the seeded daily). Quality grades: essences and basic materials (ores, hides, herbs; not relics or gear) come in 5 grades, Faint/Lesser/Plain/Greater/Pure (`GRADES`, `GRADE_M` in `data.js`). A graded item has the id `<base>@<g>` (`ess_Fire@4`, `golem_core@2`); **Plain keeps the bare id**, so SECRETS, recipes and old saves are untouched (secrets only match Plain; a graded stand-in logs why). Use `gradeOf`/`baseIdOf`/`gradedId` (pure, `data.js`), `gradedItem(base,g)`/`essG(trait,g)` (create the item on demand) and `isEss`/`essTrait`; never parse `ess_` ids by hand. Drops roll a grade with `rollGrade(zone,heat,x)` (`GRADE_ODDS`, Heat shifts it; `gdrop`/`gess` for a zone, `dropNow`/`essNow` for the current one); `x` is a 0-1 roll so seeded daily outcomes pre-roll it (`poi.gr`, the fraction left of a shop roll). Upgrading (Forge tab, `upgrade(id)`, `UP` and `GRADE_LV`): 3 of a grade + embers make 1 of the next, with an Alchemy-skill success chance (a miss loses 1 of the 3) and an Alchemy level needed per grade. Infusion counts essences by `INF_W` (Greater = 3 plain, Pure = 9, Lesser = 1/3, Faint = 1/9) and `infPay(t)` picks what to spend. Salvaged gear gives essences of grade `sg` (set by `combine()`: the lowest grade among its inputs, Plain if none) and never more essences than were put into it (`essIn` in `crafting.js` counts them through the recipe, so essence-then-salvage is never a gain; plain gear gives none, only materials), and a deconstructed item's fragments follow its Plain traits (see Materials); infusion overpayment is kept as `S.infCredit` (ninths); `worth()` scales by `GRADE_W`. `mkGraded` in `state.js` rebuilds graded items, so `loadSave()` resets them to their fixed values. `reforge(id)` makes a new `c_` item, like old-forge tempering.
- Crafting skills: `S.skills={key:xp}` for the `SKILLS` table in `data.js` (`skillOf(item)` maps a form to a skill, `skLv(k)` gives the level from `SK_LV`, `SK_CAP` the highest result tier per level). `combine(items,{roll})` is now random per attempt (`roll` 0-100 fixes it; previews and the sim pass `roll:100`): difficulty (`CRAFT`) minus skill level gives the failure chance (`FAIL`), split into a mishap and a fizzle (`FIZ`: ingredients lost, a share of their worth refunded in embers). A result above the skill's tier cap is blocked in the forge. A clean result keeps the id `c_<hash(inputs)>` (recipe book, secrets, stacking); a mishap gets its own id and a `clean` field, and `reforge` turns it back into the clean item. The first forge never fails. Merge gains above `SOFT` count half; upgrading a crafted item of the same form adds difficulty.
- Audio: the header's speaker and music-note buttons independently cycle effects and music through Off / Low / Medium / High. `sfx(name,delay,opt)` in `audio.js` is a safe no-op without a user gesture or Web Audio; add effects in `SND` (built from `osc`/`noise`) and a gap in `GAP` for rate limiting. Combat floaters in `fx.js` pass `opt` (`el` and weapon `form`) so the hit cue can layer the right element and weapon voice. Weapon forms have different impacts, elemental weapons layer their own accent (fire, ice, poison, holy, shadow, arcane), each signature ability has a short wind-up tell, and misses, wards, blocks, parries and incoming elements have their own cues. The shared mixer adds a short stereo room, compression and music ducking for major events. `music.js` generates the original score live. Each region (`MUSIC_SCENES` keyed by zone id), the forge, regular battles and bosses has its own eight-bar piece with its own key and mode, chord progression, melody, lead instrument (flute, bell, horn, glass, pluck) and texture (Frostfang wind, Cinder Hollow embers, the forge anvil, combat percussion). A new region needs a `MUSIC_SCENES` entry with the zone's id, or it falls back to the woods theme. `syncMusic()` follows game state at each render and switches arrangements on bar lines. Audio has its own random streams, so it never changes game rolls. Everything works offline from `file://`, with no music downloads or dependencies. Preferences (`on`, `vol`, `music`, `musicVol`) stay in `localStorage` key `everforge-sound`, never in `S`; an older muted preference starts music muted too. Playback starts after a gesture, pauses with a hidden tab, and releases finished voices.
- Lifetime counters live in `S.stats` (`statsDef()` in `state.js`; a counter a save does not have takes the default). Count in the central places (`strike`, `dealt()` before any `B.hp-=`, enemy hit path, `enemyDown`, `youFall`, `finishDaily`, `gainEmbers`/`spendEmbers`) and show them in `renderStats()` (Codex tab).

## Player goals and the opening loop

Fresh saves start with a readable fight that drops Ember Coal and food. Deconstruct the coal for guaranteed Fire fragments, make the guaranteed first craft, then equip the fire weapon and step onto empty ground in the Woods for a fight that highlights its burn damage. Exporting during either opening fight does not strand the tutorial: the next eligible map step resumes it. Older saves skip this sequence. The goal strip groups tutorial steps into chapters.

Recipes and revealed legendary rumors have a **Pin goal** action. The expandable tracker shows held ingredients and possible sources in unlocked regions; unknown rumor ingredients stay hidden until hint scrolls reveal them. Source links open the corresponding region, while travel stays disabled during an encounter or daily run. Pins persist in saves. After the tutorial, choose between two hunt bounties and one crafting bounty, with rewards shown before acceptance. Choices survive reloads, and progress starts on acceptance. Existing accepted bounties continue as before.

Guard highlights its empowered Attack and ready abilities. A successful empowered hit gets a brief confirmation, and known elemental weaknesses appear as build opportunities. These cues do not change combat rolls or damage rules.

`node tools/smoke.js [steps]` plays the game headlessly through the real UI (Playwright + Chromium) and fails on any page error or if the run gets stuck. It plays twice: a **fresh** save played naturally (no forced unlocks, zones, Heat, daily or peddler: systems open by their own rules and the bot clicks the intro cards' Got it), and an **unlocked** save (a fresh state with every `UNLOCKS` entry open and read) that carries the feature checks gating would keep out of a 400-step newcomer run (abilities, Heat, daily, buying, selling, upgrading, reforging, fleeing, being hunted, every affix, tiles). Both runs deconstruct with *Deconstruct all* and forge a random pattern or blueprint from the Forge's Patterns panel (auto-filled when picked); the natural run must open Patterns, deconstruct and forge from a pattern, and every won affixed fight must drop an affixed raw that deconstructs into that affix's essence.

### Balance simulator

`node tools/sim.js` runs the real combat and map code (`startBattle`, `attack`, `parry`, `ability`, `useItem`, `pstats()`, `step()`...) headlessly in Chromium, with `render`/`save`/`fx`/`sfx`/`toast` stubbed. It builds player kits by actually crafting with `combine()` (the kit table at the top of `tools/sim.js`: balanced `melee`, `defensive`, `staff` (a staff and a shield, Arcane stacked) and a min-maxed `stacked` kit at woods/pass/hollow/spire "end", plus a `starter` kit, the fresh-save sword; every recipe is checked with `checkMix` and any warning is printed) and prints each kit's `pstats()`, essence use (in plain-essence equivalents) and the embers to upgrade its graded essences. It fights every enemy at each Heat with three bot policies: `passive` (attack only), `basic` (attack, guard on a telegraphed special) and `smart` (choose Guard or Parry from expected damage, status risk and survival, use the ability when ready, heal below 35% HP). For a direct comparison, `--policy guard,parry` uses the same ability/healing policy while always choosing the named defense against specials. The codex is pre-mastered for the kit's zone and earlier ones (`--no-mastered` to turn that off).

The main pairing is **arrival**: the kit of the previous stage against the next zone (starter kit against the woods); **on-stage** (the kit's own zone) is a second table. Skills: each stage kit assumes the crafting-skill XP in `STAGES[..].sk` (a focused player's skills at that point); the sim builds the clean result (`roll:100`) and reports the crafts, expected attempts and expected fizzles a player would need. Per row: win%, mean turns and mean HP left% per Heat. Flags (Heat 0 unless noted): `TOO_EASY` (win 100%, over 85% HP left and under 4 turns, under 10 for a boss), `TOO_HARD` (wins under 50%) and `SLOG` (mean turns over 30, 50 for a boss, any Heat). `--attrition` adds expeditions of 4 regulars and a boss on one HP bar (35% heals after fights 2 and 4) with clear %. The economy section plays whole expeditions through `step()` on the grid (BFS to POIs, then the lair; steps, steps in the dark and torch left are reported) (enemies roam through the same `foesAct`; the `by foe` column counts fights an enemy started) and reports embers, essences and items per expedition, with and without the salvage value of the new loot (the bot never salvages), and the peddler's price range.

**Those tables hold the hand-made enemies only.** The ten wild creatures of every region (`w_<zone>_<i>`, `monsters.js`) are no longer mixed into the matrix, the win% grids, the kit-strength table or the flagged cells: they have their own sections below. (Older runs mixed the two, so those numbers changed; the economy and attrition runs still meet the wild creatures on the map, as the game does.)

```
node tools/sim.js                                  # full matrix, 40 fights per cell, about 45 s
node tools/sim.js --n 100 --seed 7 --attrition --out sim.json
node tools/sim.js --kit stacked --zone spire --heat 0,5 --policy smart
node tools/sim.js --enemy wolf,imp --elite          # --elite adds a magic (+) and a rare (*) variant of each regular enemy
node tools/sim.js --rarity --seed 7 --n 100         # the rarity table (see Affixes), no matrix and no economy
node tools/sim.js --fresh --exped 6 --seed 3        # the first expeditions of a new save with the unlock gates on
node tools/sim.js --wild --n 10 --seed 7            # only the wild creatures and the Rift masters
node tools/sim.js --wild --rift-seeds 1,2 --rift-depth 1,3   # the Rift rolls to test
```

`--rarity` prints the **rarity table**: the arrival pairing (each kit against the next zone's regular enemies at map row `--row`), every enemy, with every affix in turn (the first affix cycles through all that species can roll; a rare's others are rolled), win%, mean turns and HP left for normal, magic and rare at Heat 0, 3 and 5 for each policy, plus a win% per first affix. It flags `RARE_HARD` (smart play winning under 85% at Heat 0-3) and `SLOG`. The target: a rare is a real fight but smart play with a stage kit still wins over 85% at Heat 0-3. It covers the hand-made regulars only: the wild creatures' rarity rows are in the wild section. `tools/smoke.js` also runs every affix once through the real UI, three rare combinations, and fails on a stuck fight or a missing essence.

**The wild creatures** (`--wild`, or in a default run) are printed per creature, kit, policy and rarity. **native** is the creature as the game rolls it: its own rarity, its own affixes and the stats the game gives it. The forced rows (**normal**, **magic**, **rare**) are what-ifs: the stored stats (the game keeps a wild creature's stats with its own rarity divided out) go back onto the region band, and the affixes are rolled like a regular's, so the rows compare like with like (`--wild` and `--elite` add them). The second table is per region and biome (the regions come from `ZONES`, so a promoted biome shows up on its own), with the mean over the region's ten creatures, the **Δ vs regulars** (the difference against the same zone's hand-made regulars, same kit, policy and Heat: `Δ win` in points, `Δ turns` in turns, `Δ HP` in points of HP left) and how many of the region's creatures are flagged `TOO_HARD` or `TOO_EASY`. A win% difference alone reads `0` whenever both sides win 100%, so the turns and the HP left carry the comparison. A forced `magic`/`rare` row is compared with the regulars **of the same rarity** (the `--elite` rows, which `--wild` and `--elite` now also compute behind the scenes); without such a row the Δ is `-`, never a Δ against the normal regulars. An empty cell (no wild result for that region, kit, policy or Heat) prints `-`, not `0%`. In the `--out` JSON the region table keeps the three deltas as `wildByRegion[].heats[].delta = {win,turns,hp}`. `--wild` prints only these two sections and the Rift one; the regular matrix is still computed behind them (the Δ needs it) but not printed.

**The Rift masters** (`--rift-seeds`, `--rift-depth`, in the same sections) roll a region from a seed: by default one seed per Rift biome (the biome is the first draw of `rng(hash("sim-rift:"+s))`, so the rolls are stable across runs), and the master fights every `*/spire` stage kit at Heat 0 (the Rift has no Heat) with the Rift not mastered. **A boss gets no affixes at all** (`startBattle` skips the affix block for every boss), so the `native` row is the master as the game fights it today: no affixes and about 1.2× the region's HP band, while the `magic` and `rare` rows apply the master's own affixes to the fight (sim-only) to show what it was rolled for. `--rift-wild` adds the ten wild creatures of each roll. The **Rift summary** at the end lists the master by biome and by depth (mean win% of the native rows) and, with `--rift-wild`, the Rift's own wild creatures the same way, with the counts of the `TOO_HARD` and `TOO_EASY` cells.

`--fresh` plays the first `--exped` expeditions of a new save (starter kit, first `--policy`) with the unlock gates on, and prints per expedition the POIs on the map, what the bot met, and what opened afterwards; every other mode opens all unlocks first (and learns every technique except Precision, which only lowers the failure chance, so the "attempts needed" numbers are the ones from before research), so its numbers do not depend on the unlock order. `--fresh` also plays the research chores of a newcomer after each expedition (salvage spare gear, bind essences into the sword, study up to three families, learn the cheapest technique it can afford) and prints the RP earned per expedition and the first expedition whose total reaches each technique's cost.

Flags: `--n` fights per cell, `--zone`/`--enemy`/`--kit`/`--heat`/`--policy` (comma lists; `--kit` matches names like `melee`, `spire`, `staff/pass`; `--zone` keeps enemies of those zones and the kits whose own or arrival zone is listed), `--seed` (seeds `Math.random` per cell, so results are reproducible), `--row` (map row 0-5 used to scale regular enemies, default 3), `--all` (also print the other pairings), `--attrition`, `--no-mastered`, `--jobs` (browser pages in parallel), `--exped` (expeditions per zone), `--trace` (print one battle log per selected cell instead of the matrix, e.g. `--trace --kit defensive/spire --enemy archon --heat 5 --policy smart`), `--no-econ` / `--econ-only`, `--out` (JSON path), `--wild` (only the wild and Rift sections, with the forced rarity variants), `--no-wild` (skip them in a default run), `--rift-seeds` / `--rift-depth` (the Rift rolls and depths to test), `--rift-wild` (the Rift's own wild creatures too, on with `--wild`), `--force` (the forced variants to trace). Change the numbers in `data.js`, rerun with the same `--seed` and compare. **Every number quoted in `README.md`, `tasks.md` or `todo.md` comes from `--seed 1`** and is labelled with it; the default seed is random, so a run without `--seed` cannot be reproduced.
