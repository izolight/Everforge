How to play

The map

The loop. Cross each region's map and fight for raw finds. Deconstruct them into base materials and essences, forge gear from patterns, bind essences into it, and beat the boss at the end of the map to open the next region. Past the Spire the Wanderer's Rift waits: every expedition there is a random region, and each one goes deeper than the last.

The map. Each region is a grid of tiles. Tap a glowing tile next to you to step; you only see a couple of tiles around you, and explored ground stays dim. Walk onto fights and chests to meet them, and elites (magic or rare: tougher, with affixes that change the fight and drop essences), shrines and peddlers and mystery tiles (?). The boss waits in the lair at the top, behind a camp. Losing sends you back to a fresh map; cleared regions can be explored again.

Returning foes. Regular map monsters return after 30 map steps while they are outside your view. Elites and bosses do not respawn. Daily maps are excluded.

Torchlight. Your torch burns down one step at a time. When it runs out you only see one tile around you and every step risks an ambush. Camps, shrine prayers and campfire rests relight it.

Health. HP carries over between fights. Heal by eating, resting at a campfire (one charge per three wins), camps and shrines. Healing traits on gear mend you each turn.

Defeat. You wake at the region's edge with full HP, and a scavenger takes one unequipped item.

Fighting

Abilities. A weapon's form gives it one signature move (a spear lunges, a staff channels its element, claws rend) and a shield can bash. They show as extra buttons in battle, take your turn, and then need a few turns to cool down. Inspect the item to see the numbers.

Trait effects. Fire burns, Ice can freeze, Poison stacks, Holy heals you, Shadow drains life, Sharp raises crits, Heavy pierces armor, Swift gives extra strikes, Steam makes enemies miss.

Enemy moves. Specials are announced a turn ahead. Guard cuts every hit in the enemy's action by %, blocks new statuses, and grants Braced: +% damage on your next Attack or ability. Braced does not stack; existing statuses still tick. Parry negates an announced special and ripostes, or interrupts a heal. Its chance starts at %, rises with special training, Speed and weapon form, and caps at %. The button shows your current chance. Study improves training up to ranks. A failed or mistimed parry means +25% damage and a lost guard.

Armor. Defense cuts the damage you take by DEF/(DEF+), with diminishing returns, and Defense, resists and Stone together never cut a hit by more than %. Your own blows lose armor/(armor+half your strike) to the enemy's Defense, so a weak attack still gets through. Magic counts times as much as Attack in your strikes.

Statuses. Some creatures burn, chill, poison or curse you. They tick or hinder you for a few turns and end with the battle. Medicine gear, resist gear and some consumables (Medicine, Holy, Water, Nourishing) shake them off.

Knowledge. You learn a weakness by hitting it, a resistance by being resisted, a special move by surviving it, stats by winning and drops by finding them. Study reveals one fact but costs your turn.

Gear and forge

Gear. Seven slots. An off-hand weapon adds a weaker second strike; a shield can block hits.

Mixing. Patterns turn base materials into gear. In the crucible only sensible combinations work; the Recipes tab has the table. Deconstructing raw finds gives essences, which mix with everything. Salvaged gear gives back half its materials.

Infusions. Pure essences can be infused into yourself in the Gear tab. The trait stays with you whatever you wield.

Previews. Known recipes show their full result before you forge. New combinations show the form and any reactions you've discovered. Every forge can go wrong: the line under the crucible shows your skill for that kind of item, the odds of a clean result, a mishap or a fizzle (the ingredients are lost, some embers return), and the highest tier that skill can reach. Failing teaches a little, but easy recipes teach less. Merged trait values above 8 grow at half rate, and upgrading a crafted item again is harder each time. A higher skill and simpler recipes make failures rarer. From Smith Lv 3 the forge also appraises the traits to expect.
